# Development Journal — Enemy AI Combat

**Time Spent:** ~3 hours

## Overview

Today I continued developing the enemy AI and started building a more complete enemy combat system.

The main goal was to make the enemy detect the player, chase them, enter attack range and perform attacks with proper timing and cooldowns.

A big part of today's work was also debugging the problems that appeared while building this system.

## What I Implemented

### Enemy AI States

I continued working with a state-based AI system.

The enemy currently uses four main states:

```text
Idle
Chase
Attack
Dead
```

Each state has a specific responsibility.

The enemy starts in `Idle`, detects the player and switches to `Chase`.

When the enemy gets close enough, it can switch to `Attack`.

After the attack is finished, it returns to `Chase`.

The `Dead` state prevents the enemy from continuing its normal behaviour after dying.

### Enemy Chasing

I implemented the basic enemy chasing behaviour.

The enemy calculates the direction and distance to the player and moves toward them while they are within the detection range but outside the attack range.

The enemy also rotates toward the player while chasing.

This created the basic foundation for the enemy's melee combat.

### Enemy Attack System

I implemented the basic enemy attack flow.

The enemy can start an attack when the player is within attack range and the attack cooldown has finished.

The attack uses a short delay before damage is applied.

The general flow is:

```text
Player detected
      ↓
    Chase
      ↓
Attack range
      ↓
Start attack
      ↓
Attack delay
      ↓
Check player distance
      ↓
Deal damage
      ↓
Return to Chase
```

This gives the attack its own timing instead of instantly dealing damage.

### Attack Cooldown

I added an attack cooldown so the enemy cannot attack continuously.

The cooldown controls when the enemy is allowed to start its next attack.

While the cooldown is active, the enemy can continue its normal behaviour instead of becoming stuck.

### Attack Direction

I also changed how the enemy handles its rotation when attacking.

Instead of continuously rotating toward the player during the entire attack, the enemy turns toward the player when the attack begins.

The attack then keeps that direction.

This should also make it easier to connect the attack system with future attack animations.

### Attack State Protection

I added an `isAttacking` flag to keep track of whether the enemy is currently performing an attack.

This prevents the enemy from starting multiple attack routines at the same time.

It was especially important because the attack uses a coroutine and has a delay before the damage is applied.

## Problems I Encountered

### Enemy stopped after attacking

While building the attack system, I encountered a problem where the enemy could stop after attacking.

The problem was caused by how the attack cooldown was being checked.

I changed the logic so that the cooldown only controls when a new attack can start instead of preventing the enemy from continuing its normal behaviour.

### Enemy kept rotating during the attack

The enemy was initially continuing to update its rotation toward the player while attacking.

This made the attack direction unstable.

I changed the attack behaviour so the enemy turns toward the player at the beginning of the attack and then keeps that direction.

### Player could escape during the attack

Because the attack now has a delay before damage is applied, the player can potentially move away during that time.

I added a distance check before the damage is dealt.

If the player has moved outside the attack range, the enemy does not deal damage and returns to chasing.

### Enemy pushed the player

During testing, I noticed that the enemy could push the player when it was already close enough to attack.

The reason was that the enemy was still moving toward the player even though it had already reached attack range.

I changed the chase behaviour so the enemy stops moving once it enters attack range.

This prevents the enemy from constantly moving into the player's collider while waiting for another attack.

## What I Learned

Today's work helped me understand how different parts of an AI combat system interact with each other.

I learned more about:

* State machines
* Detection and attack ranges
* Attack cooldowns
* Coroutines and delayed actions
* Preventing multiple actions from running simultaneously
* Controlling enemy rotation
* Checking conditions during an attack
* Separating movement and attack behaviour

One of the most useful parts of today's work was debugging the AI after implementing the basic combat behaviour. Instead of only adding features, I had to observe how the enemy behaved in-game and then figure out why it was getting stuck, rotating incorrectly or pushing the player.

The enemy now has a functional foundation for basic melee combat, which can later be expanded with more advanced combat mechanics and animations.
